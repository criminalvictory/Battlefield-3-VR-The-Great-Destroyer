# M1014 (Benelli M4): a held gun, its shells by hand

Built 2026-11-18 on branch `worktree-wf_a3a04c64-f0e-2`, from the measurements made the same day in Semper Fidelis
(`build\survey\M1014_*`, the mesh dumps `build\mesh_13377_0_*`, `mesh_324_13377_*`, `mesh_450_13701_*`). **Nothing of this has
run in the game yet**: the game was not to be started for this work. Every offline test passes; the in-game list is at the end.
The counting core and its design are `src\shotgunammo.h` and `docs\research\shotgun-shells.md` (unchanged here; this note is
what was added on top).

The game's own reloads (`M1014_partial.bin`, `M1014_dry.bin`):

- **Top-up.** The gun is rolled; the left hand comes up from 24 cm below with a shell, nose up 63 degrees at the port's mouth
  (base (0 0.005 0.452)), tilting forward as it rises (52, 35, 27, 15, 3 degrees at bases (0 0.031 0.462), (0 0.050 0.471),
  (0 0.066 0.480), (0 0.071 0.504), (0 0.077 0.530)), and is hidden with its base at (0 0.080 0.535). One shell every 0.5 to
  0.6 s. This is the way in the VR version uses, point for point (`shellload::kM1014`).
- **Fired dry.** The bolt locks 6 cm back 0.4 s after the last shot. The left hand fetches a shell 35 cm out to the left and
  lays it over the top into the ejection port: base (0 0.104 0.463), nose forward, by 1.2 s. The bolt goes home at 1.4 s (the
  release) and takes the shell; then four shells from below at 2.3, 2.8, 3.3, 3.9 s.
- At rest the game parks its shell on the lifter (base (0 0.077 0.422)) and hides it for 0.3 s after each shot.
- The handle is never pulled by the game. There is no sound of it in either reload wave.

## 2. What the VR version does

**As a held gun** (the same tables every held gun has):

- `skin.cpp`: a `kHeldKinds` row (frame 3, up 0, sights, muzzle, port, palms, depth 14151, no magazine table), a `kHeldParts`
  row for the dots, a `kHeldEyes` row (eye z 0.198, the ghost ring's housing at z 0.333), an eleventh row in
  `ClearRifleSpread`'s table (its GunSwayData from Venice-EBX; buckshot 300 m/s and flechettes 450 m/s for the pellets' start).
- `tools\recoil\kick.tsv`: body 13377, shape `m1014`, its kick **recorded**: 7.15 degrees at 94 ms, 0.2 cm back (the row was a
  physics placeholder of 3.1 degrees and 2.3 cm). `src\recoilguns.h` is the generator's.
- `src\gunbodies.h` (the generator's): its body for free hands, holsters and pickups; `gunprofile.h`'s name; the collision
  shape `build\guns\m1014.bin` (bolt left out; new file, not in the repo, as every shape).
- With the recoil row filled and a body, the hands know it: the holster by its slot, the hand-over of a taken gun, recoil and
  buzz from its own data.

**Its shells by hand** (`src\shellload.h/.cpp`, pure; `ammo.cpp`'s tube; `hands.cpp`'s `TubeByHand`; `input.cpp`):

- A fresh squeeze (grip or trigger) of the hand that doesn't hold the gun, at the belly, gives one shell, held by its base in a
  fitted three-finger pinch (`src\shellposes.h`). Let go of at the belly it goes back; anywhere else it falls and lies 30 s.
- **Loading port.** The shell's nose brought to the mouth under the receiver, nose up or forward (within 60 degrees of the
  game's own 63), goes onto the game's own way in and from then on moves only as the hand pushes it (the hand's shell
  projected onto the way; the glove rides the shell). The last 1.5 cm it goes by itself: the latch's click, one more in the
  tube, written into the game's count. Pulled back before that it is in the hand again and goes in only after it has been
  taken 6 cm away. Let go of on the way it falls out. A full tube: one knock, it stays in the hand.
- **Ejection port**, only with the bolt held open on an empty chamber: the shell brought within 4.5 cm of the port, nose
  forward, lies on the carrier (drawn there). It is counted when the bolt takes it.
- **Bolt release.** A hand arriving on the button (its middle or an open hand's index or middle fingertip within 3 cm, having
  moved at 15 cm/s or more in the last 0.15 s) with the bolt held open sends it home: the port's shell chambered, else one
  from the tube, else home empty. Not for 0.3 s after that same hand laid a shell into the port; once a visit.
- **Bolt handle.** A fresh squeeze within 7 cm of the knob takes it; the bolt follows the hand back. From home, drawn 85% of
  its 6 cm and let go: racked (a chambered shell is thrown out as a loose live shell and is lost to the count; the next is
  fed, or the bolt stays open on an empty tube). Held open: a tug of 6 mm and let go sends it home. While it is held back the
  trigger fires nothing.
- **Firing.** Each pull fires once (the game's own). The last shell leaves the bolt drawn back 6 cm (the game's own bolt went
  home: its count keeps the hidden round). The trigger then fires nothing: with the bolt home on an empty chamber one click,
  then silence until the bolt is worked.
- **The hidden round.** The game's count is the mod's plus one, so the game never reloads by itself. X held asks for the
  game's own reload: the hidden round is taken out at the next look (R is sent 60 ms after the hold for that), each shell of
  the game's is kept, a shot ends the asking. A reload not asked for is given back when its count has stopped rising. X
  tapped does nothing (it sent R before). The asking itself is `src\tubeask.h` (pure, `tools\tubeasktest.cpp`).
- **The key is the shotgun's own** (after the review). The reload key (X held, the mod's R, the keyboard's) counts for the
  shotgun only while its slot is the hands' slot and it is the gun drawn in the hands, from a press begun there
  (`tubeask::KeySince`, the time kept on the tube). Before, the key was read for every gun: fired dry, put away, the other
  gun reloaded and the shotgun drawn again inside 3 s, its hidden round went out and the game reloaded it by itself (A10).
  A key still down as the shotgun comes into the hands asks nothing until it has been seen up (A11); X let go is told on
  every let-go, and a forgotten tube takes "X held" with it (a swap or a death with X held left it set for good).
- **Swapped away by the game.** The game's own pickup (R) puts the shotgun into a pickup with its hidden round in the count
  (a hand's take writes the count without it). `ammo.cpp` remembers that pickup, the gun's name and the count; the gun that
  comes back out of it with the same count is met as already carrying the hidden round (`tubeask::Meet`, A12), not a shell
  richer. Guarded by the count: a pickup whose count changed is met as any other. Not run in the game.
- **Forgotten.** Where the tube is forgotten (a new life, a checkpoint, a swap, out of the slots, `tubebyhand 0`) and where
  the hands are turned off, `shellload::Clear()` is called: no shell in a hand, no handle held, the bolt as the next count
  has it. (Without the gun in the hands `shellload::Update` already dropped all that after 0.2 s; what stayed was a
  checkpoint loaded with the gun still in the hands.)
- **The last shell with the hidden round out.** Found by the asking's test: X tapped with one shell left and that shell
  fired inside the asking's 3 s left the game's count at 0, and the stand-in game began its own reload before the mod's next
  look (2472 times in 2000 random sessions). So the trigger's pull on the last shell with the hidden round out ends the
  asking and waits, a frame or two, until that round is back in (`input.cpp`, `ammo::TubeEndAsk`): 0 times with it.
- **Either hand.** The hand at work is whichever doesn't hold the gun; the right's hold is the left's flipped. The ports and
  the button are where they are on the gun for both.
- **Drawn.** The bolt group at its place on the frame while it is back; the game's parked shell is not drawn (only during the
  game's own reload); the gun's own shell draw is issued again for a shell in a hand, on the way in, lying in the port, and
  for each loose shell (in both passes: the depth pass's bone 4).
- **Sounds**, all from the game's files: `shell_in_m1014` (the click in the tactical wave's loop, 0.755 to 0.835 s),
  `port_in_m1014` (full wave, 0.965 to 1.040), `bolt_m1014` (full wave, 1.105 to 1.325), `dry_m1014` (the shared trigger
  click), `shell_land_v0..7_m1014` (the game's shotgun shell landing). The handle drawn back is silent: the game has no sound
  of it and none is borrowed.
- **Switches** (weapon.txt): `tubebyhand 0` gives the shells back to the game; `shelltest 1/2/3` a scripted left hand for
  films with nobody in the headset (a shell up the port; the port load and the release; the handle); `shelllog 1`.

## 3. What is forgiving

- Racking always feeds from the tube, and "tube then release" works (the core's rules; the real M4 feeds only when the hammer
  falls or the drop lever is pressed).
- The way in is 12.4 cm long (the game's own), entered within 3 cm of its first 3 cm, and the hand may stray 9 cm from it.
- The port takes a shell within 4.5 cm, turned up to 60 degrees off the bore.
- The release is pressed by a hand's middle as well as a fingertip, from any side.
- A shell let go of anywhere costs nothing: it was never counted.

## 4. What could not be measured or checked

- **Nothing was run in the game.** The hold, both loads, the release, the handle, the count's writes, the sounds and the buzz
  are untested there. The test list below is the first thing to do.
- **The level has bare arms** (Semper Fidelis, The Great Destroyer: draws 18108/16053, no gloves). No held gun of the mod's
  table has been in bare hands before. As the code stands each bare arm goes whole to its controller with the game's own
  fingers; with a shell in the left hand the bare left's fingers are set to the pinch (as the pistol's holds are). Whether the
  right hand sits on the pistol grip and the left on the fore-end as they should, and whether the pinch fits the bare hand
  (it was fitted on the glove at width 1.15), only a film shows. The bare right hand has no finger table: with the gun in the
  left hand the right carries a shell with the game's own fingers.
- **The zoomed sample looked 0.78 degrees up** (one draw scan). The dots' own mesh says the line is level; `up` is 0. If shots
  land low of the dots at 20 m, that sample was right: `up` becomes -0.78.
- **The palms** are the helper's, about 2 cm true; the free hands' learning takes the game's own when it sees them rest.
- **The handle and release holds are not fitted.** On the handle the hand closes to a fist where it took the knob; on the
  release it is the hand as the controller has it.
- **The pinch at the port** stays the carry's; the game's own thumb-on-base push was not taken (bare-arm skeleton).
- **The game's reload with a count written into it** (shotgun-shells.md 7.17): does its loop go on when the hidden round is
  taken out as X is pressed, and does 60 ms suffice. Watch for "the hidden round out of its count" followed by "shells kept".
- **A checkpoint can make a shell** (shotgun-shells.md, section 6's open problem): unchanged, shared with the pistol.
- **The longer tube** is taken from a count of 7 or 8 seen in the gun; a part-used one met at 6 is read as a full short one.
- **Spent hulls** stay the game's own effect (no casing row).
- **Its landing sound's set**: the outdoor takes everywhere; the game's own choice indoors was not read.
- **Its clips on a PC that already has sounds.** The launcher and the DLL make the mod's clips only when `build\sounds` holds
  none at all, so an update never brings a new clip: on such a PC the twelve `*_m1014.wav` are missing and the shells are
  silent (the code plays a clip only if it is there). On this PC they were made with the new `gamesounds.exe --sounds ...` and
  put into `build\sounds` (new files). For a release the "once" rule needs to become "whenever a clip of the table is
  missing"; not changed here.
- **Lying upright.** Dropped near upright the gun came to rest propped on its grip's bottom and its tube's end (both as
  wide as it is) in 8 of bodytest's 195 drops. `loose.cpp` now tips a gun that would go to rest top up on a base under 4 cm
  across (rolled about its length toward the side it leans to, harder each time, six times at most): bodytest has no
  exemption any more, 0 of 195 stand, and every other gun's rows are the same to the digit (the tip acts only where a gun
  would otherwise have gone to rest so, which no other narrow gun does). In the game it will show as the gun pausing
  propped for up to a second and then going over: to be looked at in a film.
- **Bare arms and the shells.** The shell in the hand goes by the controller's glove (its bind space, which the bare arms
  share) and the port by the gun's frame: neither uses the held gun's palms, so the palms learned from the bare arms (another
  piece of work) change nothing of the shells themselves; they move where the two hands sit on the gun. What the shells
  still lack on a bare-arms level is as written above: the pinch was fitted on the glove, and the bare right hand has no
  finger table (the gun in the left hand: the right carries a shell with the game's own fingers).
